## Scenario "reservoir" (legacy)

The uplift_sandbox relief-reservoir model, in metres and years:

    dR/dt = U(t) − k Rⁿ,   k = U_peak / R_limⁿ,   R_eq(U) = (U/k)^(1/n)

RK4, 2000 steps over 2 T. Verified against the legacy km/Myr series (1e-12 m), a scipy
reference (2e-4 m), float32 (1 mm), and dt (0.4 m between 400 and 4000 steps).

## Scenario "columns": two lockstep columns A / B

The illustration device of DESIGN.md §3: a weak column A and a column B with a resistant
body, under one U(t) and one ELA(t). Per column, elevation above a fixed base level:

    dz/dt = U(t) − E_sub − E_gl

**Column A** is the landscape's relief reservoir: `E_sub,A = E_ref = k z_Aⁿ`.

**Column B — the asserted coupling (a switch).** B's rock resists an erosional demand
`D_B`:

    E_sub,B = D_B · f(r_B, c),    f(r, c) = 1 / (1 + r (c − 1))

where `r_B ∈ [0, 1]` is the resistance at B's surface and `c` the subaerial contrast. What
sets `D_B` is the one real design decision of the tier, and it is the hypothesis-B question
itself, so the page offers three couplings side by side:

| coupling | `D_B` | while protected | after stripping |
|---|---|---|---|
| **landscape** (default) | `k z_Aⁿ` | B lowers at `E_ref/c`; upland grows at `E_ref(1 − 1/c)`; lifetime **T·c/E_ref** | `E_B = E_A`: the upland **freezes** |
| **own relief** | `k z_Bⁿ` | B is its own reservoir with `k/c`: equilibrates at `c^(1/n) R_eq` and then erodes at U; lifetime between T/U and T·c/U | relaxes back to A |
| **max** | `k max(z_A, z_B)ⁿ` | landscape while B ≤ A, own relief above | bounded, decays |

Tested: landscape with A at steady state strips the body at `T c / U` with the upland at
`T (c − 1)`; own relief with c = 16 equilibrates at 2 R_lim; max stays below the landscape
freeze; defaults are unchanged by the switch.

The landscape coupling encodes "a plateau's erosion rate comes from its own low relief, not
its elevation", which is the physical content of hypothesis B; own relief encodes the
reservoir logic that a column at steady state erodes at U whatever its erodibility, under
which persistence needs great thickness. Two consequences of the landscape coupling to keep
in mind when reading the plots: the column cannot destroy an upland once the body is
stripped (that is marginal retreat, d2_across), and when A is glacially capped its
subaerial rate is small, so a protected B climbs far above A. The readout compares body
lifetime with event duration, the timescale statement of DESIGN.md §3.

**The body in the material frame** (`core/lithology.js`): a horizontal layer with top at
material height `z_0 − d` and thickness `T`, evaluated at `z_m = z_B − U_cum(t)`; soft edges
of 10 m keep RK4 smooth across the boundary. The body is exposed when B's cumulative
erosion equals `d`, independent of the uplift rate (tested at U = 1 and 4 mm/yr). The
elevation panel draws the body in the present frame (`z_0 − d + U_cum`), so it rides up
with uplift and B's surface line enters and leaves it.

**Elevation-dependent glacial erosion (hypothesis A).** The column asserts the ice as a
"buzzsaw" window round the ELA, Gaussian below it and with an optional floor φ above it
(deep ice keeps eroding high ground):

    E_gl(z) = P · g(z),   g = exp(−½u²) for z ≤ ELA,   g = φ + (1 − φ) exp(−½u²) for z > ELA
    u = (z − ELA(t)) / w,   ELA(t) = ELA₀ − A_ELA · ½(1 − cos 2πt/P_ELA)

applied per column at its own elevation, and reduced on B by `f(r_B, c_g)` with its own
glacial contrast. With `P ≫ U` a column is capped on the lower flank of the window where
`E_gl + E_sub = U`:

    z_cap = ELA − w √(2 ln(P / (U − E_sub)))

(tested to 20 m). Consequences worth knowing when reading the plots:

- With φ = 0 a resistant column whose reduced glacial rate `P / c_g` is below U climbs
  *through* the window and escapes above it, where the Gaussian gives no erosion. That is
  a property of the symmetric window, not a claim about ice above the ELA.
- With φ > 0 the floor `φP/c_g` acts at any height; if it exceeds U no column can escape
  (tested with φ = 1). This is the hypothesis-A ingredient "planation under deep ice"
  in one dimension.
- The ELA cycle moves the cap up and down; with `A_ELA` comparable to `w` the column is
  planed over a band, not a line.

**Numerics.** RK4 on the vector `(z_A, z_B, U_cum, E_cum,A, E_cum,B)`, 2000 steps over
2 T. Checked: uniform columns reproduce the reservoir exactly; `z = z_0 + U_cum − E_cum`
to 3e-11 m; 500 versus 8000 steps differ by 7 m across the layer discontinuity.

## Sources

  observations, replaced by computed ice in d2_along and d3.
